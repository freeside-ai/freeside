# Wave 6 Planning: Adjudication Contract Splits and Loop Decomposition (#835)

The wave-6 spine planning session (tracking #835) executed §7's standing
assignment — "the spine assigns its contract splits at wave planning"
for the #697 finding-adjudication design — and decomposed the
convergence-and-yield row into sixteen units. This note records the
split and sequencing decisions and their rejected alternatives; the
schedule itself lives on #835 and the unit issues.

## Decisions

- **The #697 contract splits resolve to a nine-link serialized chain
  whose first five links are adjudication-critical by construction:**
  #679 (finding location/severity) → #702 (cross-round identity) →
  #836 (FindingAdjudication artifact and vocabulary) → #837 (item type
  through sync) → #703 (typed disposition binding), then #838 (shadow
  records), #692 (readiness verdict class), #839 (yield history), #713
  (bundle hardening). Ordering rationale: the engine lane (#840–#844)
  cannot start until artifact, item type, and disposition binding are
  merged, so those five run first and the engine lane opens as early
  as the serialization allows; #838–#713 gate only the shadow wiring,
  the yield surface, and the exit run. The rejected alternative — one
  omnibus adjudication contract unit — was declined on the unit-size
  budget (a migration, goldens, a sync-carried item type, and MockServer
  parity are four amplifiers in one PR) and on wave-5 review history
  favoring persistence-first and daemon-versus-sync seams (#652/#657
  precedent).
- **The loop lands happy-path-first as four serialized engine units
  with explicit interim behavior:** #840 (deterministic routing; fast
  path parks pending the remediator, residue parks to attention) →
  #842 (remediation and automatic re-review; the dormant
  `PutFindingDisposition` join gets its first real producer) → #843
  (adjudicator annotation site, replacing the park-everything residue)
  → #844 (yield-driven convergence). Each interim state is safe by
  §7's own fallbacks (parking is the ceilings' not-accepted case), so
  every intermediate merge leaves the daemon coherent. Rejected:
  landing the adjudicator before the remediation loop — judgment
  without a fast-path loop to route into inverts §7's structure, where
  the deterministic path is the normal work and the model is residue.
- **The shadow record contract is front-loaded as its own chain link
  (#838) instead of waiting for a mid-wave insertion.** Wave 5 absorbed
  three mid-wave contract-chain insertions (#720, #732, #740) for
  migration needs discovered at implementation start; the shadow arm's
  never-satisfies-review distinction is a near-certain migration plus a
  trust-boundary re-gate, so it is scheduled now. Revisit if the
  implementation plan finds the existing record shape sufficient: the
  unit then shrinks rather than the chain reordering.
- **Wave-5 audit boundary conditions were evaluated against code, not
  carried forward as prose:** #713 staged (reviewer instructions still
  compose through `codex_explicit_bundle_v1`,
  `daemon/internal/exec/review_instruction.go:17`); #692 staged
  (readiness reaches sync through #839, the recorded trigger); #688,
  #690, #691 not staged (no wave-6 unit adds a caller of the guarded
  readiness surfaces).
- **"The full chain on the real backlog" and the 1B.0 exit are
  close-condition activities on the tracker, not units,** following the
  wave-5 exit-run precedent: they produce evidence and an evaluation,
  not a PR.

## Revisit When

- Wave-6 convergence measurement shows credible, material, in-surface
  findings routinely reaching the model residue: revision 31's recorded
  miscalibration condition; the exit evaluation checks it.
- The contract chain's head stalls while the engine lane idles: the
  first five links are the wave's throughput bottleneck by design, and
  a stall there is a schedule problem, not a dependency error.
