# One Card Width; Standing Notices in the Detail Column

The owner decided on 2026-10-09, as item 7 of the 8 Oct interfaces handoff,
that every card in the Mac detail column draws at 640pt at any pane width
and that the standing notices draw at the top of that column. The decision
arrived as a brief in chat that names its target as option 1a of a frame
called `Wide Pane - 9 Oct 2026.dc.html`. That frame is not in the
repository and the agent could not open it, so this note is the record of
the decision, and the choices below that a frame would settle are the
agent's reading of the brief.

## What the Owner Decided

- **The two-column decision card is retired.** Chose one 640pt column at
  any pane width over the 1,000pt two-column card of
  [the two-column note](2026-10-08-1322-two-column-inspector.md) (frame 7.8,
  sweep 3b, #1802). This supersedes that note's first replaced rule and its
  360pt right column, and the line of handoff item 4 that has the two-column
  card fill the pane. Controls sit under the last module and above the
  hairline folds. That note's Evidence pointer and its reading of "one pane"
  stand.
- **Finding adjudication keeps its findings stacked above the act.** The
  owner's reason: reading every finding is the precondition for accepting
  them all, so the act stays at the bottom of the stack and does not move
  beside it. Past two findings the primary label carries the count
  (`Accept All 6 Dispositions`).
- **Standing notices leave the window-wide slot at regular width.** The
  freshness banner, the stopped indicator, and the prompt-history save
  warning draw at the card's x and width at the top of the detail column,
  so the sidebar is never under a notice. Compact width keeps the
  full-width slot above the list.

## Choices the Agent Made

- **Two findings keep `Accept All Dispositions`.** The brief puts the count
  on the label "when findings > 2". The recommendation's sentence already
  states the count at two.
- **An empty state in the detail pane centers in the card's column, not in
  the pane.** The brief's finish line lists the empty state among the
  surfaces that are 640pt wide at one x. Chose to center it horizontally in
  that column and keep it vertically centered over seating it top-leading
  like a card: it is not a card, and handoff item 4 keeps it on the pane
  ground. The seat is an explicit option the detail pane's states pass, and
  the default stays centered across the pane, because the same view also
  draws in lists, readers, and sheets wider than the column. A regular-width
  iPad detail page takes the column seat too, as its cards already do.
- **The content under a notice takes a module gap, not the pane's top
  margin.** Chose an explicit top margin passed from the column to its
  content over an environment value, and a pure predicate (`isShowing`)
  over measuring the notices' height. The predicate asks each notice the
  question its own body answers, on the notices' own schedule, so the seat
  never lags a frame behind a notice that appears; a test pins that the
  predicate and the drawn height agree in every freshness state.
- **The six-second decision receipt stays where it was,** first in the
  detail column and above the notices. It is not a standing notice and the
  brief does not name it.
- **An open inspector narrows the card, not the notices.** The inspector
  attaches inside the decision detail, below the notices, so in a window
  too narrow for both the card shrinks beside it while a notice keeps the
  column's 640pt cap and runs on over the inspector. Chose that over
  measuring the card's pane to cap the notices: a notice speaks for the
  whole column, the window-wide slot it left also stood over the
  inspector, and the brief's widths assume a closed inspector. This is the
  one state where a notice and the card under it differ in width.
- **The content keeps one place in the tree with or without the
  notices.** Collapsing the sidebar moves the notices to the window's
  slot under an open card; a column that branched on having notices
  rebuilt that card, which dropped its selections and reported it opened
  twice. A test pins one appearance across the move.
- **A collapsed Mac sidebar is read from the split view's column
  visibility.** That is the brief's Mac compact case. iPhone and iPad are
  unchanged at every size class, because the iOS app has no detail column
  beside its list.
- **Diff blocks needed no change.** The diff reader already scrolls a long
  line inside its hunk block, and no card draws a diff.

## Revisit When

- The 9 Oct wide-pane frame lands in `docs/design/`: check the empty
  state's seat and the notice spacing against option 1a.
- The owner wants a notice to match a card narrowed by its inspector:
  the notices then need the card pane's measured width, or a place inside
  the decision detail beside the inspector.
- A card needs a fact and its act visible together on a short window: the
  one column is then the constraint to argue against, not the pane width.
- The iOS app gains a split layout: the notices then have a detail column
  to draw in.
