# Unit Size Budget Rises to 1,500 Authored Lines

The owner chose on 2026-11-07 to raise the Unit Sizing budget from 1,000 to
1,500 authored changed lines, over keeping 1,000 or moving to 2,000. The
budget stays soft. This note amends
`devlog/2026-08-18-0818-unit-size-budget.md` and
`devlog/2026-10-05-1014-unit-sizing-revision.md`.

## Changed Assumption: The Review-Round Evidence Is Retired

The 1,000 figure rested on Codex review rounds, which multiplied above
1,000 lines because each fold push re-reviewed the whole diff. That note
said to revisit when the reviewer, review trigger, or fold workflow
changed. The owner retired the Codex reviewer on 2026-10-21
(`devlog/2026-10-21-0848-retire-codex-reviewer.md`), and neither
retirement note revisited sizing.

- **The slope had already flattened.** For PRs merged 2026-09-26 to 10-20,
  Codex finding-reviews by authored size were 1.00, 0.51, 1.23, and 2.32
  (under 500, 500 to 1,000, 1,000 to 2,000, over 2,000), about 2× from the
  smallest to the largest bucket against August's 6×.
- **The mechanism is mostly gone.** Force pushes before approval fell from
  about 1.4 per PR to 0.3 once Codex left. The pre-push reviewer runs before
  a PR opens and leaves no forge trace.
- **Owner review load is not visible on the forge.** Approval is a label.
  Ready-to-approved time is flat by size, but 65% of approvals arrive in
  batches of three or more within five minutes, so it measures queueing.

## Evidence for 1,500

240 PRs merged 2026-10-24 to 11-07. Authored lines as Unit Sizing now
counts them: regenerated paths and mechanical commits left out (eight PRs
carried a mechanical commit; four of them fell below 1,000). "Sent back"
is a red head before the merged head; "handed back" is a lander
`needs-human`; both come from `scripts/landing-measures.py`.

| Authored lines | PRs | Sent back | Handed back | Pushed before approval |
| -------------- | --- | --------- | ----------- | ---------------------- |
| under 1,000    | 169 | 5%        | 6%          | 16%                    |
| 1,000 to 1,500 | 30  | 3%        | 3%          | 23%                    |
| 1,500 to 2,000 | 12  | 17%       | 25%         | 33%                    |
| 2,000 to 3,000 | 18  | 6%        | 22%         | 17%                    |
| over 3,000     | 11  | 27%       | 9%          | 73%                    |

- **1,000 to 1,500 showed no rise in send-backs or hand-backs** (one each
  in 30 PRs), though pushes before approval rose from 16% to 23%.
- **Above 1,500, most buckets ran higher,** but not every measure in every
  bucket (2,000 to 3,000 had 6% sent back). The 1,500 to 2,000 bucket rests
  on 2 to 4 events in 12 PRs, so the data can't separate a 1,500 knee from
  a 2,000 one.
- **Fix issues citing a PR showed no size effect** (about 10 in all).
- **Forced rebase conflicts peak at 1,000 to 2,000** (48% of those PRs sit
  in a pair on one of the 20 most-conflicting paths), but they
  concentrate in three shared file groups that any PR touching them hits;
  #2756 tracks removing them, which helps more than splitting.

## Mechanical Commits Leave the Count

Chose to leave the lines of a commit listed in `.git-blame-ignore-revs`
out of the count, beside regenerated output. AGENTS.md (Commits) already
keeps moves, renames, and formatting in such a commit, so the test needs no
new judgment. #2567 counted 4,426 lines; its move commit holds 4,357.

## Estimates Are Recorded Against Actuals

Over-budget PRs in the window had estimates two to four times low (#2634:
1,200 to 1,600 estimated, 5,051 measured; #2491: 1,000 to 1,400 and 4,581;
#2452: about 1,500, with 3,717 production lines alone). Nothing fed the
error back. A PR whose issue carries an estimate now records its actual
count beside it, so the plan-wave reference step has pairs to calibrate
against. Without that, a higher figure would mostly raise actual sizes.

## Rejected

- **Keep 1,000.** Its evidence is retired, and 1,000 to 1,500 showed no rise
  in landing failures.
- **2,000.** Defensible if PRs that size don't cost the owner more review,
  but this window's 1,500 to 2,000 bucket ran worse than smaller units on
  every measure, if on few events.
- **Drop the budget.** Landing failures still ran higher above 1,500,
  and the split seams and amplifier list stay useful at planning.

Unchanged: the soft-not-gate rule, the scheduling-door hold after a
proposed split, the amplifier list, and the split seams.

## Limits of the Evidence

Force pushes include an author's own rebases. The authored count includes
tests (45% of lines) and design handoffs under `docs/`. One two-week
window; no significance testing. The analysis was a one-off, so #2755 adds
the size breakdown to `scripts/landing-measures.py`.

Revisit when #2755's breakdown covers the next wave: move to 2,000 if the
1,500 to 2,000 bucket is no worse than smaller units, and lower the figure
if units of 1,000 to 1,500 start failing at landing more often.
