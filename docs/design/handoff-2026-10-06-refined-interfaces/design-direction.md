# Freeside design direction (visual-audit series, Sept–Oct 2026)

Keep Freeside's identity while making the path from understanding to action much clearer. Yardstick for every client surface; the 2 Oct handoff (D01–D09) is its first application.

P1 **Reading order.** Lead with the actual question, finding, status, or verdict. Make the proposed outcome and next action easy to find. Supporting history and technical detail further down.
P2 **Typography carries hierarchy.** Serif for meaningful inbox summaries, no added bold. Keep the keyword-style section headings and the existing font system. Reduce repetition before making text smaller.
P3 **Show less initially, keep it accessible.** Source identifiers, digests, bindings, routine context and detailed evidence belong behind clearly named disclosures. The initial view contains enough to understand the decision.
P4 **Consequential information stays visible.** Failures, waivers, stale state, concerns, uncertainty and action restrictions remain prominent. Passing checks can be compact. "Unverified" stays visible; its explanation is on demand.
P5 **Group by what the person is deciding.** A finding and its proposed disposition belong together, with rationale and alternatives attached to that item. Question options are visibly distinct and easy to compare.
P6 **Containers selectively.** Independent findings, alternatives and important hold reasons get clear separation. Ordinary supporting sections rely on spacing and headings. No layers of similar boxes giving everything equal weight.
P7 **Actions prominent, scope explicit.** Actions sit close to the information they concern. Say whether approval applies to one item or the whole group. Stacked on iPhone, adaptive on Mac, behavior preserved.
P8 **Readability and familiar controls.** Keep readable task rows, filters, selection treatments and right-aligned urgency/time. Long messages expand in place without losing text or attachments. Empty forms look empty, with persistent labels and distinguishable placeholders.

Aim: a calmer first view, stronger emphasis on meaningful content, a predictable route to deeper detail, in Freeside's existing visual language.
