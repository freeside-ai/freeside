Design language survey — nine dialects, nine ways to say look here, three grammars, one recommendation, eighteen patterns accounted for, twenty-eight surfaces drawn as built beside refined
The surfaces on main as of 6 Oct share tokens, faces and chips, but the same job is done several ways depending on which unit built it: the daemon's reason is boxed under a label on ten cards and unboxed, folded or dropped on the other four; a section is headed in mono, sans or serif; "more" is a keyword disclosure, a sentence disclosure, a pill or a link; a claim is marked four ways; the wash-and-bar container means four things. Part 1 lays the dialects side by side, each specimen drawn from a built screen; Part 1b does the same for emphasis — how each surface currently says "look here" — and proposes one ladder. Part 2 recomposes one card — ready_for_final_review, the one that mixes the most — under three candidate grammars. Part 3 is the recommendation and what each component would change, with the rows that need an owner decision marked.
Direction · the yardstick every rule below is measured against · design-direction.md
P1Reading order
Lead with the actual question, finding, status or verdict; outcome and next action easy to find; history and technical detail further down.
P2Typography carries hierarchy
Serif summaries without added bold; keyword section headings and the font system stay; reduce repetition before reducing size.
P3Show less, keep it reachable
Identifiers, digests, bindings, routine context and evidence behind clearly named disclosures; the first view is enough to understand the decision.
P4Consequence stays visible
Failures, waivers, stale state, concerns, uncertainty, restrictions prominent; passing checks compact; Unverified visible, explanation on demand.
P5Group by the decision
A finding and its disposition together, rationale and alternatives attached to that item; options visibly distinct and comparable.
P6Containers selectively
Independent findings, alternatives and important holds get separation; supporting sections rely on spacing and headings; no layers of equal boxes.
P7Actions prominent, scope explicit
Close to what they concern; say whether approval covers one item or the group; stacked on iPhone, adaptive on Mac, behavior preserved.
P8Familiar controls
Readable task rows, filters, selection and right-aligned urgency/time stay; long messages expand in place; empty forms look empty with persistent labels.

Proposed — one ladder, one treatment per rung
The eye should fall in the order P1 names: what is asked → what was said or found → what to do → what to beware → everything else. Each rung gets exactly one treatment, and the face does most of the work: serif is for meaningful statements — anything a party said or the daemon concluded that the operator must actually read. Boxes are demoted to a structural job (this is one item among several). The quote splits into its two existing cuts and each gets one meaning: the gray rule on a soft wash is the agent's voice — options, claims, summary, messages — so the unverified register has a shape as well as a label; the accent bar on the accent wash is the system addressing you — the hold, a scope conflict, the selected row. The chat bubble becomes that same quote, agent on the left and you on the right.
The agent summary moves from rung 8 to rung 2: serif at text size, no added weight, quoted, the keyword still above it. Nothing is boxed to achieve it.

Item or quote? The finding card decides it
A finding holds two voices. The reviewer's message was authenticated by the daemon; the model's proposed route was not. Quoting the whole card would put the authenticated message in the agent's voice, so the finding stays a bordered item (rung 6) — one of several you compare — with the message in plain serif, and only the proposal inside it is quoted (rung 5a). On the question card the whole option is the agent's words, so the whole option is the quote. Test: is every word in the container the agent's? Quote it. Otherwise border it and quote the agent's part.
Reading order inside a finding: the message leads at statement size; the proposed route follows in ink inside the quote — the quote marks it, so the accent is not needed and would pull the eye to the proposal before the finding. Accent on text means a link or attention, nothing else.
On the question card the agent's own recommendation is the compact mark (R21): the bare keyword trailing the OPTION line, in the accent because it is attention, with no ⓘ of its own — the card's one ⓘ covers it — and that ⓘ sits on the eyebrow, because the question itself is the agent's (R27). It must not interrupt label → tradeoff, which is the comparison. Answer and Retry is the default route, so it takes the fill; the card gets its third stop. The blocker line ("Implementation is blocked until this is answered") is gone: it states what every agent question states, so it earns its place only when it says more — another stage blocked, a scope conflict, a deadline. The stage itself is in Run and binding details.
